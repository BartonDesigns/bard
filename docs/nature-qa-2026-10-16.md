# Nature QA: where two things meet

The owner asked for "a nature QA pass for realism... go way beyond this, and look at connecting areas that are off". This pass looked at everything in nature where two things meet and could look wrong (the ground and what stands on it, one ground and the next, near and far, wind in the trees and in the grass, light and shade), engine-wide, outside the water and the shore (the WATER & SHORELINE agent's) and the night lights (the NIGHT LIGHTING agent's).

## How it was looked at

A browser harness (session scratchpad `landseams/audit.cjs`) loads `island/dev.html` in headless Chromium (software GL), goes to each spot, lets the world settle by frame count, pauses the game's own frame loop and renders a ring straight from the renderer: four headings at eye level (1.7 m) and two from a low angle (0.45 m), at 10:30 so the shadows read. The base build was served from a copy of origin/main's bundle, this branch from the worktree, so every capture has its pair. A live page (`landseams/repl.cjs`) took targeted captures, turning one thing off at a time to find each cause, and counted what stands where.

Captures come from the canvas's own pixels. The drawing buffer carries alpha (anything drawn with alpha to coverage writes it), and the page shows the canvas over black, so each capture is flattened over black the same way; read raw, foliage looks paler than it is.

Spots: the island village and its forest; the Bay suburbs (San Ramon); the Diablo foothills; a redwood grove (Muir Woods); Svalbard; a desert (the Sahara's Tassili); an alpine valley (the Bernese Oberland); Kyoto (its regional kit town had not built its lots within the settle time in software GL, so the captures there are of the hills it stands in); MAGMA, TERRAN and ICE.

The machine was shared with several other agents' browsers throughout (load average above 20 on four cores): a capture took a minute or more on Earth, a new spot's first several minutes while its shaders compiled. The ring was kept to six captures a spot.

## The fixes

- **Grass** (`world/grass.js`): the glow and the sheen are gathered apart and added after the lights, times how much of the sun reaches the fragment (three's own light loop with the sun's shadow kept, `SUNLIT_BEGIN`) and times the canopy's shade (for when shadows are off). A tuft whose foot is within a stride of the eye shrinks away. The parting round your feet only happens at a walker's eye height over the ground.
- **Leaves** (`world/vegetation.js swayMaterial`): a back face takes no specular.
- **Wild ground** (`nature/wildground.js`): every plant, stone and bit of litter asks for its own spot (`onPaving`: the drive roads in the cell with their sidewalks and a margin, and the coarse land map's paving and roofs). The hot desert's shrubs and grass thin with the rain; under about 200 mm they keep to the dry washes.
- **Rocks** (`nature/rocks.js`): a damp dark band at the foot of every wild rock, at the depth each layer sinks its stones by (`wildground.js footAt`), wandering with the rock's own grain. (`world/lodfade.js` now calls a material's own `onBeforeCompile` with the material as `this`.)
- **Ground snapping** (`world/groundsnap.js`, new): `baseHeight` (the lowest of five samples under the footprint, less a sink, never more than a stride below the centre, NaN ignored), `baseFromSlope` (the same from a slope already measured: no extra samples), `slopeTilt` and `tiltFromGradient` (rocks and low plants lie with the slope). Used by the region's flora (trees by trunk, shrubs and tufts by spread; grass and ferns lean with the slope), the globe's woods and the island's scatter. No allocation.
- **Trees on graded roads** (`bay/berms.js roadDelta`, `gradeVersion`; `bay/city.js placeTrees`; `bay/streettrees.js`): within 300 m the trees, stakes, grates and leaf drifts take the grade's lift or cut at their foot, and are placed again when the grading grid is redone (every 100 m of travel).
- **Globe ground** (`earth/globeterrain.js`): the photographs' relief at 1.5 cm and fading sooner, their grain a quarter either way; the slope's aspect (`shadeSide`, pole-facing by hemisphere) shifts the snow's air by 2.5 degrees and the 25 m mottle breaks the line; scree below the crags; moss on shaded wet rock.
- **Snow** (`world/terrain.js`): roughness 0.74, 0.34 on the scoured ice.
- **Gusts** (`world/vegetation.js GUST_GLSL`): the grass's cat's-paw field, the same hash, scale and speed, read per tree; between the patches a tree takes about a third of the gust, under one up to 1.4 times.
- **Sunbeams** (`world/sunrays.js`): `sunLit` eases into the open value over the box's outer eighth, and toward its far end.
- **Guards** (`world/terrain.js SAFE_GLSL`, used by `HEIGHT_GLSL` and so the island and planet ground, grass, litter and caves; the far ground; `bay/terrain.js gradedHeight`; `earth/globeterrain.js`): `safeH(h, fallback)`. `uCell` guarded against zero. `bay/berms.js` clamps each delta to 60 m and writes 0 for a NaN.

## Hills drawn in facets from a distance (added after the owner's review)

The owner: "reduce polygonals that are visible in hillside structure from a distance". The
ground's far rings were power-law grids, whose spacing at their inner edge jumped well past the
ring inside them. The Bay's far ring (out to 95 km) had cells of 270 m at 4 km and 480 m at
10 km, against the near ring's 60 m at its edge. That is some 3 degrees, 40 pixels or more on a
laptop's screen. The globe's middle ring had 2.7 degrees at 6 km and its far ring 3.2 degrees
at 40 km (a phone's far ring, with no middle ring, 9 degrees at 6 km). Each vertex's normal was
also taken over a fraction of its spacing (the Bay's far ring: 60 m slopes at vertices 480 m
apart), so the shading aliased into a blotch per triangle as well as the shape being faceted.

- `world/terrain.js ringGrid` (new): a square grid whose cells grow in step with the distance
  from its inner edge, so every cell is the same size on screen; a few coarse steps cover the
  middle, where the ring inside draws. `withSpacing` gives a grid `aGsp`, its spacing at each
  vertex.
- The Bay's far ring (`bay/terrain.js FAR_GRID`): 320 segments on a desktop (256 on a phone),
  fine from 3.5 km, just inside the near ring's 3.9 km hole however the two rings are snapped.
  Cells of 89 m at 4 km, 220 at 10 km and 440 at 20 km on a desktop (1.3 degrees); on a phone
  110, 280 and 560 (1.6 degrees). Its normal is taken over half its own spacing.
- The globe's middle ring: 224 segments fine from 4.2 km (inside the fine ring's hole, with the
  rings' middles led ahead of you by up to 450 m): 1.3 degrees throughout. Its far ring: 192
  segments fine from 36 km (1.3 degrees; it was 3.2 at 40 km). A phone's far ring: 160 segments
  fine from 5 km (3 degrees; it was 9 at 6 km). The globe's normals and the octaves of its
  height already went by the grid's spacing; they now read it from `aGsp`, not a power-law
  formula.
- Cost: the Bay's far ring on a desktop has 103,000 vertices (66,000 before). The globe's
  middle ring and a desktop's far ring are unchanged; a phone's far ring has 26,000 (16,600).
- Tests: `island/tools/hill-facets.test.mjs`.

## Cost

- GPU: the grass fragment adds a minimum and two lengths in the light loop and three smoothsteps per vertex; the sway vertex adds one value noise (four hashes) per vertex of every plant; the globe ground fragment adds about thirty operations and one value noise; the rock fragment four. No new textures, no new passes, no new draw calls.
- CPU: the city's trees are placed once more each time the grading grid is redone (once per 100 m of travel; the same pass already runs every 40 m), the street trees' stakes likewise. The wild ground checks each item against the cell's drive roads and one map lookup when the cell is first made. Region flora, globe woods and the island's scatter use the slope they already measure. No per-frame allocation.
- Phones: nothing new is on by tier; the changes cost the same everywhere and are small.

## Tests

`node --test island/tools/nature-qa.test.mjs` (11) and `island/tools/hill-facets.test.mjs` (3, the far rings): the foot on a slope never above the ground round its footprint and never sunk out of sight over a cliff, NaN ground ignored; the slope tilt; every GPU-lifted ground guarded; the sunbeams' soft edge; trees beside a graded road riding a measured cut, and no NaN into the grade; the grass's glow shadowed and its tufts kept off the lens; the trees' gusts on the grass's own field; nothing of the wild ground on paving; the globe ground's grain, snow and rock; every wild rock's foot.

## Not done, for whoever is next

- A real town in Kyoto was not captured (the settlement had not built in time in software GL).
- The Bay's survey against the globe's coarse ground is untested at eye level.
- Snow does not drift against walls and fences; house foundations meet the snow with a hard line on ICE.
- Mid-range trees have no shadows (one shadow box).
