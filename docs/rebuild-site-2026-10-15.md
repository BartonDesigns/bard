# The rebuild site, polished (15 October 2026)

Every item a crew brings to a damaged building, rebuilt to one standard: in proportion, of the right materials, worn, lit by night, animated, laid out from the street it is on, and cheap to draw. Engine-wide: everything is in `island/src/structures/` (no scene edits), and every site anywhere gets it.

## Files

- `crewkit.js`: the parts builder and the one material. Every vertex carries what it is made of (`aMat`: roughness, metal, kind, wear) so one draw holds painted metal, galvanised and worn steel, chrome, rubber, glass, wood, concrete, sheet glass, lamps, beacons, reflective strips and decals. `kitMaterial()` adds the live sky reflections the cars use (`withCarSky`), grime worked in by wear and height above the ground, lamps and tail lamps lit by night (and by `uLamps` while a truck drives), rotating amber beacons, retroreflection toward the viewer by night, red aviation lights, work-light lenses, wheels and the mixer's drum turned in the vertex shader (`uSpin`, `uDrum`), a hook's cable let out (`uStretch`), a dithered fade (`uFade`, complementary for crossfades so nothing pops) and a height clip (`uClipY`, the scaffold rising and struck). Its shadow material does the same fade and clip. The decal atlas (`crewAtlas`) holds each company's door mark, side panel, fence banner, drum band, boom name and counter-jib banner, and the signs (hard hats, PPE, DANGER keep out, site office, visitors, chevrons, a plate, the toilet, reversing caution). The cloth atlas holds debris netting and welded fence mesh.
- `crewtrucks.js`: the vehicles. An American conventional cab (hood with a rounded nose between broad fenders cut round the wheels, chrome grille and bumper, split windscreen, west coast mirrors, sun visor, roof marker lamps, beacon bar, stack, air cleaner, fuel tank with steps, door seams, handles and the company name). On chassis rails: the tri-axle dump truck (ribbed steel body, cab guard, tailgate with its caution sign, a lift axle up, heaped rubble), the mixer (drum on its pedestals tilted to the hopper, banded in the company's colours in a spiral, chute, ladder and platform, water tank), the boom truck (knuckle crane folded behind the cab, timber deck, headache rack, stake pockets, chevron edges; bars, blocks or glass). The glazier's high roof van with its A-frame rack and panes on the kerb side. The all-terrain crane's carrier (four axles, low cab on the left, outrigger beams in reflective stripes, jacks and pads). Tyres are lathes with shoulders and sidewalls (a multiple of four sides so a corner is the contact point), dished rims with nuts, twins at the back. Every company has the same kinds; each kind is built once per company and load, near and far, and shared.
- `crewcranes.js`: the mobile crane's upper works (superstructure, operator's cab, counterweight slabs with reflective squares, hoist drum, four telescopic box sections to the head and its sheaves, luffing ram, the company's name on the boom). The tower crane: footing with fixing angles, square lattice mast (corner chords, zigzag diagonals, struts, a ladder inside with landings), ties back to the facade every 18 m when tall, slewing unit, cab, A-frame head with pendants, triangular lattice jib with walkway, counter-jib with walkway, rails, hoist, cabinet, ballast and banner, three red aviation lights. The hook block on two falls of cable; loads on slings (bars, blocks, glass, a rubble skip, a concrete bucket); the same loads alone for where they wait or were set down.
- `crewsite.js`: mesh fence panels (round galvanised frames, rubber feet, clamps, stays, banners tied on the street side), the gate swung open, water-filled barriers (with a lamp), cones with two reflective collars, the debris box (roll-off skip) with ribs, doors and rubble, the site office (on blocks, ribbed walls, framed windows with grilles, door with its sign up three steps with a rail, air conditioner, signs), the portable toilet, the light tower (generator trailer, mast, four aimed lamps), sand and gravel heaps, lumber, scaffold boards, cement sacks, wheelbarrow, generator, stock by the stage.
- `crewfolk.js`: the crew, one instanced draw (and one shadow) for every worker on every site, posed in the vertex shader. A proper figure: hips, tapered torso, vest with reflective bands and braces, neck, head, ears, hard hat with brim, peak and ridge, upper arms, forearms, gloves, thighs, shins, boots. Per worker only position, yaw, task, its clock, tool and looks (skin, hat, vest, shirt, build). Tasks: walk, carry a block, carry a bar on the shoulder, carry a pane of glass upright, hammer (slow draw, quick strike), shovel (dig, lift, turn, throw), guide a load with an arm up, hold the mixer's chute, lay blocks on one knee, talk, stand by. Tools appear only in the hands of whoever uses them. Walkers' steps follow the ground covered.
- `crewplan.js`: the layout in plain numbers (tested): the site frame, the kerb from the nearest drivable surveyed road's own line and width (else a 6 m pavement), which way the near lane runs (kerb on its right), the fence line (a pavement kept where there is room, taken up to the kerb where there is not), side returns stopped short of neighbours, faces with room for scaffolding, the gate, the tower crane's place (a side yard, that side then unscaffolded and the mast tied to the building; else the front strip; else inside the footprint), a free spot for every item (not in a neighbour, the road, the scaffold, the gate, the walk along the scaffold or on another item; turned to fit), the trucks' kerb slots, the drive in (lane, pull in), the drive past and back in, the drive out (round a crane in the far lane), speeds, and the crane's lift cycle.
- `rebuild.js`: the orchestration (schedule and stages as before), per site: the kit for the stage (crossfaded at a stage change), the cloth (fence mesh and netting), the scaffold built once to full height and revealed by its clip, the cranes and their lifts, the trucks arriving, parked, leaving, the crew's roles and paths, the light towers' real light, the glow sprites, the sounds, LOD, the departure when the work is done, `info()`, `anchors()`, `cost()`.
- `audio.js`: `loop(kind)` for the mixer's drum, a crane's motor (whine rising as it works) and a reversing beeper, through the sound bus, synthesised.
- `index.js`: one change, the night passed on to the crews.

## The site

- Kerb and trucks: parked parallel at the kerb (centre 1.45 m out from it), each truck pitched and rolled to the ground under its wheels; the mobile crane cribbed level on timber under its pads (thicker where the ground falls away), wheels just off the road. A taper of cones from the lane to the kerb ahead of the trucks and cones along their road side. Slots: the first truck by the gate, the second behind it, the mobile crane beyond the site downstream; the office in the street past the crane if there is no room for it inside.
- Fence: Heras-style panels on the fence line with the company's banner on every third panel, the hard hat, PPE and DANGER signs either side of the gate, barriers on the pavement at the gate.
- Scaffolding: 2.1 m bays, 2 m lifts, inner standards 0.35 m off the facade, outer 1.45 m; sole boards and base plates, ledgers, transoms, five boards to a lift, toe boards, two guard rails, rails across open ends, ties into the facade every other lift every third bay with anchor plates, a zigzag of face braces, ladders up the end bay turn about; front and back runs carried over the corners where the sides are scaffolded, the sides stopping at their inner line (no gap, no overlap); debris netting on the outside, a sheet a lift high, bowed and dropped a little between the ties. It starts once the rubble is cleared, rises a lift at a time a little ahead of the frame and comes down top first at the end.
- Stages: clearing (dump truck, debris box full, wheelbarrow, generator, shovellers, the crane lifting rubble skips); frame (mixer backing in where the road ahead is clear, boom truck of bars, bars, lumber, boards, cement; the crane lifting bars; hammering at the frame's top; a worker at the chute; bars carried on the shoulder from the truck); cladding (boom truck of blocks, three pallets, cement, sand, boards; blocks lifted; layers on one knee on the walls' lift; blocks carried in); glazing (boom truck of glass on its A-frame, the glazier's van, glass frames; glass lifted). A broken window alone: the van, two glaziers, cones.
- Arrivals and departures: trucks fade in 70 m up the road and drive in with their lamps on and wheels turning, a reversing truck beeps; trucks whose part is done drive off and fade; the kit crossfades pixel for pixel at a stage change; a new site fades in when it appears in view; a finished site in view sends its trucks away and fades out before it goes.
- Night: two spot lights from the nearest site's light towers aimed at the front (none on a phone or on the governor's low tiers), additive glow sprites for every beacon, work lamp and aviation light (one draw), reflective strips on vehicles, cones, barriers, outriggers, counterweights and vests catching the light toward you.
- Sound: hammer blows as each hammer lands, the odd grinder, the mixer's drum while a mixer stands, the crane's motor, the reversing beeper.
- Workers stay inside the fence; carriers walk out through the gate onto the pavement to the truck's side and back, never into the road.
- Surfaces for the environmental graphics agent: `structures.rebuild.anchors()` lists every fence banner (world centre, normal, size, company) and every truck's door panels (in the truck's own frame with the mesh), so signage can be dressed later. The banners and door names drawn now come from `crewAtlas()` in `crewkit.js`.

## Patches (October 15, integration)

Walls broken and nothing else (structures/walls/: a fist through the drywall, a round through a partition, a blast in a room, with the building's shell untouched) get a small visit instead of a site. The crews poll `walls.damaged()` twice a second by the clock; a quarter of a game hour after the damage the glazier's van (the same van does drywall) pulls in at the kerb with one or two of the crew, who carry board in from it (the `carry` pose, not the pane) and work at the front; half an hour to an hour later the walls are put right with `walls.repair(id)`. They wait while you are within 16 m (in the room), hold off through a catastrophe like the rest, and leave a building the full crew is coming for alone. The building is found from the walls' own sources while it is built near you. `structures.rebuild.patches`, `rebuild.info()` (rows with `patch: true`). Test: `island/tools/integration.test.mjs`.

## Left for later

- The glow and floodlights are cheap stand-ins for a bloom pass; the floodlights are two for the nearest site only.
- The site does not yet clear parked cars or street furniture from the kerb slots; trucks may stand over a parked car the street puts there.
- The tower crane inside a footprint (only where neither side yard nor the front strip has room) stands through the frame's slabs.
- The crew's figure is a good silhouette, not the people/ rigs (MakeHuman bodies with skinning, wardrobes and hair: far too heavy for six to eight workers a site); faces are not modelled.
